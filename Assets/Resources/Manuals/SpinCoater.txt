<b>The Modeled Photoresist Matrix:</b> The system models a high-performance 193 nm Deep Ultraviolet (DUV) Chemically Amplified Resist (CAR) designed for printing advanced sub-micron features.

<b>Machine learning framework:</b><b> </b>MLP regression deployed via ONNX.

<b>Chemical Composition Profile:</b> At the molecular level, this wet polymer matrix consists of an organic resin base, an acrylic-based copolymer structure, pre-mixed with sensitive Photoacid Generator (PAG) molecules, base quencher compounds, and volatile casting solvents such as Propylene Glycol Monomethyl Ether Acetate (PGMEA).

<b>The Hydrodynamic Basis of Thin-Film Deposition</b><b>: </b>The physical process progresses through distinct structural phases: an initial liquid dispense phase, a spin-up acceleration phase where radial spreading begins, a hydrodynamic thinning phase dominated by centrifugal forces.

<b>Limitations of Pure Analytical Physics Solver Models:</b> While historically significant for establishing hydrodynamic principles, purely analytical physics solvers like the Emslie model create limitations because it assumes a constant fluid state and completely neglects non-Newtonian polymer physics.

<b>Industrial Empirical Scaling Paradigms:</b> To bypass the computational limitations of full multi-physics solvers, modern semiconductor fabs and resist vendors characterize recipes using empirical spin curves validated by ellipsometry metrology. The process maps <b>the final stabilized thickness (h) </b> inversely against the spin speed (RPM) via an empirical parameter channel:

<b>h = K * RPM^-n</b>

<b>The Empirical Material Parameter </b><b>:</b> A common point of confusion is interpreting the parameter  as a simple math scalar. In advanced lithography, K is a comprehensive physical constant that indirectly captures the complete chemical state of the resist, including its base polymer viscosity, solvent concentration, fluid density, surface tension, and systemic evaporation dynamics.
