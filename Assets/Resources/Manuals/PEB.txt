<size=130%><b>Post-Exposure Bake (PEB) & Acid Kinetics</b></size>

<b>Baked at 110</b><sup><b>o</b></sup><b>C for 60 sec</b><b>onds.</b>

<b>The Core Purpose of Chemically Amplified Resists (CAR):</b> In modern 193 nm DUV lithography, the exposure step alone does not alter the physical solubility of the photoresist film. The ultraviolet photons lack the energy required to break the polymer protecting groups directly; instead, they interact exclusively with embedded Photoacid Generator (PAG) molecules.

<b>The Exposure Limit Paradox:</b> If you skipped the Post-Exposure Bake entirely and placed the wafer directly into the developer fluid after exposure, the entire film would remain completely intact because exposure alone does not break the protecting groups on the polymer, leaving the resist backbone hydrophobic and completely insoluble in an alkaline developer solution.

<b>Photochemical Conversion Mechanics (Dill’s Law):</b> The exact initial spatial map of trapped acid (H0) generated during exposure is governed by a form of Dill's law:

<b> H0(x,y) = 1.0 - exp(-C * I(x,y) * E) </b>

Where C represents Dill’s activation parameter calibrated to 0.042 cm^2/mJ, which measures the photolysis rate constant and chemical conversion efficiency. The term I(x,y) represents the local normalized optical intensity map delivered by the aerial image, and E is the absolute exposure energy dose set by the user.

<b>The Hotplate as a Chemical Amplifier:</b> The primary function of PEB is to use controlled baking temperatures and durations to enhance and drive the chemical reactions initiated during exposure. The thermal energy from the hotplate forces the generated acid molecules to diffuse through the film and act as catalysts for subsequent reactions.

<b>Catalytic Resin Deprotection:</b> As the photoacid diffuses horizontally through the resist, it catalyzes the deprotection of the bulky ester groups attached to the polymer backbone. This process increases the solubility contrast between the exposed and unexposed areas, rendering the deprotected resin hydrophilic and soluble in an alkaline developer solution, which is crucial for achieving high-resolution patterns.

<b>Emergence of the Final Resist Profile (M)</b> At the conclusion of the PEB step, first-order reaction kinetics yield the finalized concentration of the remaining protected polymer matrix fraction (M). The local value of  represents the remaining chemical fraction (ranging between 0 and 1) that determines the local solubility contrast.

<b>Reading the Latent Polymer Matrix (M):</b>

<b>Yellow/Bright Regions (M = 1.0):</b> Represent fully protected, insoluble photoresist. The polymer backbone is completely intact.

<b>Dark Blue/Purple Regions (M = 0.0):</b> Represent areas where the diffused acid has successfully catalyzed the deprotection reaction. These dark zones are highly soluble and will become the physical trenches during development. 

<b>The Gradient Edge:</b> The blur between the yellow and blue regions represents the diffusion length of the acid. A wider blur indicates a longer bake time or higher temperature, which smooths out stochastic noise but worsens Critical Dimension (CD) resolution.
